## Analysis of LRR non-host rotation study from Apiary, Oregon

An unpublished exiperiment on management of LRR was setup within the Apiary area. The general design of the experiment has several treatment areas, i.) harvest Doug fir and plant Alder for 10 years then replant Doug fir (A plots) ii.) harvest Doug fir and plant cottonwood for 10 years then replant Doug fir (C plots). These same experimental designs were also replicated in other areas but the resistance species were left to grow for 20 years before Doug fir were replanted. From my understanding and interpretation, this study was never published and it sort of fell apart due to a transition between ownership of lands going form federal to private industries. Currently, new trials are going to be conducted however the alternative species that will be planted are conifers such as Coastal redwood and western red cedar. 

Site was described in previous publication (Thies and Westlind 2005). 
"The study area was a 4.5 ha clearcut on a 15% southwest-facing slope in the Oregon Coast Ranges (latitude 468000 N; longitude 123840 W); the mean elevation was 335 m. Mean annual precipitation measured near the site was 145 cm (United States Weather and Bureau, 1965). The soil in the study area was mapped as a Bacona silt loam with a surface layer of dark brown silt loam 15 cm thick. The upper layer of subsoil was 68 cm thick of reddish-brown silt loam and silty clay loam, and the lower subsoil was 68 cm of yellowish-red silty clay loam. The effective rootingdepth was 150 cm or more (Soil survey of Columbia County Oregon, November 1986). The site, site class II (McArdle et al., 1961), supported a 40-year-old naturally regenerated stand that was predominantly Douglas-fir with a lesser component of western hemlock (Tsuga heterophylla (Raf.) Sarg.), western redcedar (Thuja plicata Donn ex D. Don), choke- cherry (Prunus virginiana L.), and red alder (Alnus rubra Bong.). The understory was primarily salal (Gaultheria shallon Pursh), vine maple (Acer circi- natum Pursh) and swordfern (Polystichum munitum (Kaulf.) Presl)."

## Study description

### Description from datasheet
Threre were five treatments (A,B,C,D,E) and five reps of each treatment. Treatment plots were appoximately 1.25 acres in size. In each treatment plot there were five  0.05 acre circular measurement subplots. 
Most recent measurement was done in January, 2020. 
Treatment C: Douglas-fir initially, was 45 years old in 2020 (so 1975 start?) Thies and Westlind Apiary site was planted in 1979.
Lat & Long:   46.001997, -123.080655

### Description from email with Mike McWilliams
"all 1/10 acre plots were originally planted with 34 1-0 Douglas fir seedlings, and stem-mapped after establishment"
